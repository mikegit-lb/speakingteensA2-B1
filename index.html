A1/A2 English Speaking Masterclass

Comprehensive Lesson Content & Guided Prompts for the A2 to B1 Transition

Lesson Objective: This structured lesson plan contains 20 targeted speaking prompts designed to build conversational confidence, expand grammar flexibility, and push learners from basic communication (A1/A2) toward independent expression (B1).

Structure: The content moves from familiar concrete domains (Background) to future plans (Future), and finishes with foundational hypothetical structures (Second Conditional). Follow-up questions and linguistic tips are embedded to maximize student performance.

Vocabulary Upgrade: Bridge to B1

Incorporate these three transitional words into your answers to sound more precise and advanced.

Word 1
Pursue
verb / pəˈsjuː /
To try to achieve or follow something over a long period of time (e.g., a hobby, a career, a goal).

Use instead of "do" or "try": "In the future, I want to pursue a degree in design."

Word 2
Opportunity
noun / ˌɒpəˈtjuːnəti /
A chance to do something easily or a situation that makes it possible to achieve a goal.

Use instead of "good chance": "Moving to a new city is a great opportunity to learn."

Word 3
Independent
adjective / ˌɪndɪˈpɛndənt /
Not needing other people to help you, look after you, or tell you what to do.

Use instead of "alone" or "free": "Living in my own apartment made me very independent."

Part 1: Your Background & Personal History (Questions 1–7)
Q1. Where did you grow up, and what was your favorite part about that place?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Is that place different now compared to when you were a child?
  • Would you like to live there again in the future? Why or why not?
Performance Tip

Practice switching between "I grew up in..." (Past Simple) and description phrases like "There used to be..."

Q2. What subjects did you enjoy most during your school years?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Who was your favorite teacher and why did you like them?
  • Do you still use any of those school subjects in your life today?
Performance Tip

Don't just list subjects. Use markers like "I found history fascinating because..." to justify your answers.

Q3. Tell me about a childhood hobby that you no longer do.
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Why did you stop practicing that hobby?
  • How long did you do it before you gave it up?
Performance Tip

Use the structure "used to + verb" to talk about past routines (e.g., "I used to play collect stamps").

Q4. What is the most memorable trip or vacation you took with your family?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • How did you travel there (by train, plane, car)?
  • What specific activity made that trip so unforgettable?
Performance Tip

Use sequencing words to structure your narrative: First, Then, After that, and Finally.

Q5. Think about your first job or your first major responsibility. What did you have to do?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Did you feel confident or nervous when you started?
  • What did you learn from handling that responsibility?
Performance Tip

Incorporate the upgrade word independent here if the responsibility helped you grow up.

Q6. How did you celebrate your birthdays when you were a young child?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Was there a specific cake or gift you remember vividly?
  • Do you celebrate your birthdays differently now?
Performance Tip

Utilize past time expressions clearly: "When I turned seven...", "In those days..."

Q7. Talk about a piece of technology (like a phone or computer) that you got in the past. How did it change your routine?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • How did you manage before you had that device?
  • Was it a gift or did you buy it yourself?
Performance Tip

Contrast the past and present using "Before I got it, I spent hours..." versus "Now, it only takes..."

Part 2: Looking Ahead & Plans for the Future (Questions 8–13)
Q8. What are your main goals for your professional or academic life over the next year?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • What is the first step you need to take to achieve this?
  • Who can help you reach these objectives?
Performance Tip

This is a brilliant place to use the new word pursue (e.g., "I intend to pursue a certification...").

Q9. Are you planning to visit any new countries or cities soon? Where do you want to go?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • What kind of food do you want to try there?
  • Are you going to travel alone or with friends/family?
Performance Tip

Differentiate between fixed plans (be going to + verb) and vague desires (would like to + verb).

Q10. How do you think your daily routine will change five years from now?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Do you think you will wake up earlier or later than you do now?
  • Will technology play a bigger role in your future routine?
Performance Tip

Use "will probably" or "I think I will..." for making future predictions about yourself.

Q11. Is there a new skill, sport, or language you intend to learn in the future?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Why does this specific skill interest you?
  • How much time can you realistically spend practicing it each week?
Performance Tip

Incorporate your upgrade word opportunity here (e.g., "Learning this gives me the opportunity to...").

Q12. Where do you see yourself living when you retire? Describe the house and environment.
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Will it be near the beach, in a quiet countryside, or in a busy city?
  • What hobbies will you focus on during retirement?
Performance Tip

Practice descriptive adjectives for environments: peaceful, bustling, isolated, cozy.

Q13. Imagine you want to buy something expensive next year. How are you going to save money for it?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • What is the item, and why is it important to you?
  • Will you cut down on eating out or shopping to save up?
Performance Tip

Use structural intention markers like "I plan on cutting back on..." or "I am going to open a savings account."

Part 3: Hypotheticals & Imaginative Scenarios (Questions 14–20)
Grammar Bridge Note (Second Conditional): When handling these questions, structure your answers using:
If + Subject + Past Simple (V2), Subject + would + verb
Example: "If I won the lottery, I would buy a new house." (Note: Use "If I were" for all subjects when using the verb 'to be').
Q14. If you were the president or leader of your country, what is the very first law you would change?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • How would normal citizens benefit from this rule change?
  • Do you think it would be easy or difficult to govern a country?
Performance Tip

Ensure you say "If I were the leader..." instead of "If I was" to display solid B1 control.

Q15. If you inherited a very large amount of money tomorrow, how would you spend it?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Would you donate a portion of it to a specific charity?
  • Would you quit your current job or studies immediately?
Performance Tip

Combine your upgrade vocabulary: "If I inherited money, I would take the opportunity to..."

Q16. If you could live in any historical era (the past) or the far future, which one would you choose?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • What historical figure or future invention would you want to see?
  • What things from modern life would you miss the most?
Performance Tip

Keep your modal verbs consistent: "If I could live..., I would choose..."

Q17. If you met an alien from another planet, what would you tell them about humanity?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Where would you take them to eat their first human meal?
  • Would you feel scared or excited during this meeting?
Performance Tip

Watch out for the past tense verb format after 'If': "If I met..." (not meet).

Q18. If you woke up tomorrow and suddenly spoke five languages fluently, what would you do?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Which industries or jobs would you want to work in?
  • Which country would you visit first to try your new languages?
Performance Tip

Use pursue: "If I spoke five languages, I would definitely pursue an international career."

Q19. If you were given a chance to change one physical feature or personality trait of yours, what would it be?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • How would changing this trait alter your day-to-day interactions?
  • Do you think other people notice this trait as much as you do?
Performance Tip

Be careful to maintain hypothetical markers: "If I changed it, I would feel..."

Q20. If you found an injured, rare animal in your backyard, how would you handle the situation?
Teacher Follow-ups
  • Who would you call for professional assistance?
  • Would you attempt to feed it or keep it as an unusual pet?
Performance Tip

Ensure proper past tense realization in the conditional clause: "If I found..."
